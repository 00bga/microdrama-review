# Decisions

Numbered decisions, each in four lines: the question, the options, the measurement or reason, the decision. "Measured: no" means the decision rests on reasoning, not on a number from this project. Round-2 decisions are appended as they are made; dropped experiments stay in the list with their numbers.

## Round 1 (product)

**D1. How the model sees the cut.** Options: a video-native model that takes the whole file; frame sampling plus an image model; a transcript plus frames. Reason: a video-native call keeps audio, motion and timing in one request and needs no ffmpeg; the free tier allows it. Measured: no. Decision: one video-native call per cut through the Files API.

**D2. One process or services.** Options: one Next.js app with SQLite; a separate worker and queue; a hosted database. Reason: the brief asks for a local tool for a small team; one process on one machine is the smallest thing that can fail. Measured: no. Decision: one Next.js app, SQLite, uploads on local disk.

**D3. AI notes as drafts.** Options: AI notes land as open notes; AI notes land as drafts a strategist keeps or deletes. Reason: a strategist must own the note list, and a wrong AI note that nobody deleted would block approval. Measured: no. Decision: drafts, with keep and delete; approval gated on zero open notes.

**D4. Carried notes as copies.** Options: a note points at its earlier version; a note is copied into the new cut with a link back. Reason: a copy can carry its own fixed / not fixed verdict and its own new timecode without touching the earlier cut's record. Measured: no. Decision: copies with `carriedFromNoteId`, verdicts from the model, suggested new timecodes.

**D5. Model fallback list.** Options: one model; a list tried in order on overload. Reason: the free tier returns 503 at busy hours; the next model has its own quota. Measured: yes, in round 1 (503 storms on 3.8 Flash). Decision: `gemini-3.8-flash,gemini-3.6-flash,gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, overridable, and `--pin` for scorecard runs so a scored run never mixes models.

**D6. The bible in the prompt.** Options: no bible; the bible as a rulebook to audit shots against; the bible as a description of who the characters are. Reason (round 1): the bible is what the team judges tone against. Measured in round 1: no. Revisited in round 2 as D12.

**D7. Cost line at list price.** Options: show nothing; show the free-tier $0; show the list price of the call. Reason: a strategist deciding to run the tool on a paid key needs the number per cut. Measured: no. Decision: tokens and list price per call, with "free tier $0 billed".

## Round 2 (measurement and levers)

**D8. What accuracy means.** Options: the share of strategist notes with an AI note within 6 s (round 1); precision of AI notes by time; human-confirmed coverage of strategist moments, with a chance floor and the validity of the tool's own notes. Measurement: on the four round-1 3.8 Flash dev runs, the time-only rule scored 24 of 58 moments (41%) against a floor of 45% for the same number of evenly spaced dummy notes (`npm run eval:score --set dev` on the round-1 runs, before labels), so the round-1 number could not tell the tool from a metronome. Decision: the pre-registered definition in docs/accuracy.md: anchored moments, family-filtered matching, confirmed coverage as the headline, floors beside every automatic number, validity beside every coverage number.

**D9. Unit of recall.** Options: the strategist note; chained clusters of notes (a note joins a cluster if within 4 s of any member); anchored moments (within 4 s of the first note). Measurement: chaining merges The Haunted Crew E01 v2's 38 notes (median gap 2 s) into 4 clusters, anchoring gives 17. Decision: anchored moments, time only; note-level recall reported beside.

**D10. The split.** Options: by series; by episode; by whether a cut has a saved model run from round 1. Reason: the saved-run rule is checkable from file names and from the round-1 write-up the company holds, and it puts every cut that round 1 studied, and every cut whose notes shaped the round-1 prompt, into dev. Measured: no (a rule, not a number). Decision: dev = the four cuts with saved runs; held-out = the four with none; E04 v3 shakedown only.

**D11. Pre-registration.** Options: define the metric after seeing round-2 numbers; commit the definition, the split, the contact list and the label hash before any scored call, and push the tag. Reason: a pushed tag is a timestamp the author cannot set. Measured: no. Decision: one commit, tagged `prereg-1`, before the first scored round-2 call.

**D12. The first lever: zero-call fixes.** Options: start with the schema; start with the prompt text; start with what costs no calls. Measurement: round 1 kept only the first 12 notes (`review-output.ts`), while the model wrote 4 to 9 on 3.8 Flash; the round-1 prompt already asked for "10 to 25 notes" next to "zero is valid" and "never add a note to reach a number", and the model obeyed the dampening. Decision: lift the cap (sanity cap 60), purge the paraphrased examples, then measure the text changes (persona as a first-time phone viewer, the bible as a description not a rulebook, delivery and sound and rendering and transition lenses, density keyed on carried notes) as arm A against the purged baseline.

**D13. Two arms, attributed by mechanism.** Options: one combined change; text first and schema second as separate arms. Reason: "why the schema" needs its own row; the shot-list schema is the arm most likely to fail at the API (schema complexity, latency), so the text arm is also the measured fallback. Decision: arm A (text) and arm AB (text plus shot list per block, a block field per note), both on 3.6 Flash with the same seed, the winner confirmed on 3.8 Flash; see docs/accuracy.md P12 for the keep conditions. Measured on Flash-Lite first (the Flash models were overloaded for the whole afternoon): on the four dev cuts the purged baseline wrote 18 notes and matched 6 of 58 moments (lift 3 over its floor), arm A wrote 15 and matched 5 (lift 3), arm AB wrote 20 of which 12 were echoed carried notes and matched 7 (lift 3). Flash-Lite writes 2 to 6 notes per cut whatever the text says, so it cannot answer the density question; the arms are decided on 3.6 and 3.8 Flash. Measured on 3.6 Flash (Nov 5 and 6; 3.8 Flash answered none of its calls, see D26): the purged baseline wrote 21 notes over the four dev cuts (5, 3, 8, 5) and matched 6 of 58 moments (10%, floor 11%, lift 0); arm A wrote 20 (6, 2, 5, 7) and matched 7 (12%, floor 13%, lift -1), above the baseline on 2 of 4 cuts; arm AB wrote 23 (6, 4, 8, 5) and matched 9 (16%, floor 14%, lift 1), above the baseline on 2 of 4 cuts, with 44 to 66 shots listed per cut, every note timecode on a listed shot start (88 to 100%), boundary share 33% on E04 v1 (over the 25% line) and 0 to 20% elsewhere, generate 47 to 86 s, finishReason STOP on every cut. Neither arm reaches the keep rule (10 points of lift on one run, on 3 of 4 cuts), and AB fails its own count condition (at least 12 notes, or 1.5 times arm A, on 2 of the 3 cuts without carried notes: only Haunted Crew qualifies). The density text alone did not move the note count, and the shot list made the model enumerate the cut and still write 4 to 8 notes. Decision: both dropped; see D28 for what is frozen. No validity guard was judged for either (D27).

**D14. Windowed passes.** Options: one whole-cut call; one whole-cut call plus 40 s windows with 5 s overlap. Measurement: 1 + ceil((D - 5) / 35) calls per cut, about 25 calls per held-out run on this split, more than one day's free quota per model. Decision: not built for the Flash models this round; built as a runner mode and measured on Flash-Lite, where the quota allows it (D25).

**D15. Exemplars from the team's own notes.** Options: none; strategist notes from other episodes shown as a style sample; the same series' notes (series memory). Reason: the cheapest known fix for under-production and the product story "the tool learns the team's voice", with the leakage rule stated (other series only in the frozen configuration; series memory as a labelled extra outside the headline). Measured on Flash-Lite (Nov 6): arm A with 15 exemplars from other series wrote 19 notes over the four dev cuts (4, 3, 9, 3) against 15 without, and matched 10 of 58 moments (17%, floor 18%, lift -1) against 5 (9%, floor 6%, lift 3); the extra matches arrived with extra hit sources, not better placement. Measured on 3.6 Flash (Nov 6): arm A with 15 exemplars wrote 19 notes over the four dev cuts (7, 4, 4, 4) and matched 5 of 58 moments (9%, floor 16%, lift -7), below the baseline on every cut; the model wrote shorter notes, not more of them. Decision: dropped on both models; the exemplar section stays in the app as an option (GEMINI_EXEMPLARS) and is off in the frozen configuration.

**D16. Category `sound`.** Options: keep sound under performance; a new category. Measurement: on The Villain's Therapist E01 v1 about 10 of 24 strategist notes were about voice delivery or sound and the round-1 model wrote none. Decision: `sound` added to the enums and the UI; no migration (the column is plain text); delivery stays under performance, rendering under continuity, transitions under pacing.

**D17. Family map, one deviation from the panel text.** Options: the panel's map (performance and sound to delivery and sound; character to character and emotion); the same with emotion also reachable from performance. Reason: the prompt's performance lens is "the emotion must read at phone size", so an AI performance note about a flat expression must be able to match a strategist emotion note; without it the map would score a correct note as a miss. Decision: performance maps to delivery, emotion and sound; the rest as the panel wrote. Measured: no (fixed before any scored call).

**D18. Seed and temperature.** Options: no seed; a fixed seed; temperature 0. Reason: a seed makes a rerun of run 1 send the same request, while run 2 (seed 1000 plus the run index) is a different draw, so the published spread is the API's own variation; temperature stays at its default so the measured configuration is what the app ships. Measured: pending. Decision: seed = 1000 + run index in the evaluation; unset in the app unless `GEMINI_SEED` is given.

**D19. The ledger and the reserve.** Options: count calls by hand; a ledger that every attempt is appended to, with a stop at 15 per model per Pacific day. Reason: round 1 lost most of a day to 503 storms and a spent quota; the reserve keeps 5 calls per model for the app and for resumed held-out cuts. Measured: no. Decision: `data/eval/ledger.jsonl`, the sweep refuses to start past the reserve and stops at the first failure.

**D20. Model-aware options.** Options: send every option to every model; send thinking only to models that report thought tokens and drop an option the API rejects by name. Measurement: the saved round-1 runs show thought tokens on 3.8 and 3.6 Flash and zero on Flash-Lite. Decision: thinking goes only to models that report thoughts; a 400 that names an option drops it and tries again.

**D21. The shakedown spike on the schema and the options.** Options: assume the worst-case schema and the sampling options work; spend four Flash-Lite calls on the unscored cut to find out. Measurement: 13 cast names plus 24 aliases plus the shot list answered in 41 s with STOP; static processing, clipping and per-part resolution were all accepted; clipping keeps absolute timecodes but the model reads the end of the span as the end of the episode; per-part medium resolution did not change the input token count (25696 both ways), so that arm is null as sent. Decision: arm AB goes into step 2 with its full schema; the resolution arm is dropped unless the request-level setting behaves differently on a zero-cost Flash-Lite check; the clip finding is recorded against windowed passes (D14).

**D22. Who labels the strategist notes.** Options: the candidate labels every note by hand (about 1.5 hours); a text pass assigns the labels and the candidate checks the uncertain rows; a text pass assigns the labels with no check. Reason: the candidate chose the third, to spend the hours on the judging steps that need the video (pair and validity judgments on the held-out run). The cost is stated in docs/accuracy.md P14: the labels come from the note text alone, no one watched the cuts to write them, so the observable slice is an estimate; the headline uses all moments and does not depend on kinds. Measured: the text pass changed 38 of 138 keyword proposals (28%). Decision: text-pass labels, disclosed as such, hashed in the pre-registration.

**D23. Echoed carried notes.** Options: count every AI note as a hit source as pre-registered; drop a note written within 2 s of the new timecode of the model's own not_fixed verdict. Measurement: on Bound E04 v2, Flash-Lite arm AB restated all 12 carried notes as new notes at their new timecodes (arm A: 3 of 5), despite the instruction not to. Reason: an echo is the same claim twice; one-to-one matching stops it covering a second moment, but it inflates the hit-source count, the validity denominator and the chance floor, so keeping it would flatter nothing and penalise lift. Decision: the scorer drops echoes from the hit sources and prints their count; `--keep-echoes` restores the pre-registered reading; the held-out table is reported both ways; the app drops the same notes at ingestion so a strategist never sees a carried note twice.

**D24. A problem slot per shot (arm AC).** Options: stop at the shot list; give every listed shot a slot the model must fill with a problem or leave empty, so the decision is forced shot by shot instead of left to the model's own sense of how many notes a cut deserves. Measurement: screened on Flash-Lite on the four dev cuts (4 calls, Nov 6): the model listed 58 to 154 shots per cut and filled 1, 4, 1 and 0 slots, ending with 1 to 2 notes per cut (3 of 58 moments, 5%, floor 4%), fewer than any other arm. Decision: dropped before it cost a Flash call; the arm stays in the code as `AC` so the result can be reproduced.

**D25. Windowed passes, measured.** Options: leave D14 as a paragraph; build the runner mode and measure it where the quota allows (Flash-Lite, 500 calls per day). Measurement (Nov 6, baseline text, one whole-cut call plus 40 s windows with 5 s overlap, the clipped span named in the request, 23 calls for the four dev cuts): notes per cut 17, 18, 12 and 16 against 3 to 6 for one call on the same model; 15 of 58 moments matched (26%, Wilson 16 to 38) against 6 (10%), but the floor rose with the note count from 7% to 25%, so the lift is 1 to 2 points; time-only 59%; 5 window notes repeated a whole-cut note within 3 s and were skipped; about 480 thousand input tokens for the four cuts, about $0.18 at list price. Reason: three times the notes from the same model land no better than an evenly spaced grid carrying the same categories, so density was never the bottleneck on its own; the misses sit in clarity, story logic and pacing moments that the model does not write about at all (D13 miss lists). Decision: not frozen; reported as the measured row under "what to try next", with the quota arithmetic for the Flash models (1 + ceil((D - 5) / 35) calls per cut, 25 for the held-out set).

**D26. The reporting model.** Options: wait for 3.8 Flash as pre-registered; report on 3.6 Flash, the fallback the plan named; report on Flash-Lite. Measurement: the ledger between Nov 4 16:06 and Nov 6 17:15 (Tbilisi) shows 3.8 Flash answering 0 of 33 calls (503 or 504 every time, attempts spaced from 3 minutes to 45 minutes apart, at every hour of the day), 3.6 Flash 13 of 37, Flash-Lite 53 of 63. Decision: the held-out run is made on 3.6 Flash with `--pin`; P13 is amended before the first held-out call with this count as the reason; 3.8 Flash keeps a background probe and, if it ever answers, a 3.8 table is added beside the 3.6 one and never replaces it.

**D27. Which arms get a validity guard.** Options: 15 judged notes per arm, as pre-registered; the guard only for an arm that passes the coverage rule. Reason: the guard decides nothing for an arm the coverage rule already drops, and the judge's hours are better spent on the held-out judgments, which are the headline. Measurement: no arm has passed the coverage rule, so no guard was judged; the arm A sample (14 unmatched notes) was generated and left unjudged. Decision: the guard is judged for an arm that would otherwise be frozen; P9 and P12 are amended to say so; the validity of the frozen configuration is measured in full on the held-out run, where every hit source is judged.

**D28. The freeze.** Options: freeze the baseline, which no lever beat under the pre-registered rule; freeze arm AB, the best single number (9 of 58 against 6, lift 1, above the baseline on 2 of 4 cuts); freeze whatever looks best on the held-out cuts afterwards. Reason: P12 was written before any of these numbers existed so that a one-point difference on 58 moments could not pick the winner, and AB's edge is exactly that; choosing it now would be the author picking the best of five draws. Measurement: five levers measured on the dev set (A, AB, exemplars on 3.6 Flash; AC and windowed passes on Flash-Lite), none within 9 points of the keep rule's 10. Decision: the frozen configuration is arm P with 0 exemplars, prompt version `a8a8b0e6`, the API's default sampling, run on 3.6 Flash with `--pin`; this is `DEFAULT_PROMPT_CONFIG` in the app, unchanged. The held-out number therefore measures the purged round-1 prompt with its note cap lifted, on the model that answers; everything the rewrite added is reported as what it did not do, with the numbers. One app-eval difference is added to P15: the app falls through its model list on overload while the evaluation pins one model.

**D29. How much of the held-out run is judged.** Options: every label P9 and P10 ask for (23 hit sources for validity, 2 pairs, 8 cross-category candidates, 36 verdicts; about two to three hours of watching); only the 10 labels that decide confirmed coverage; none, reporting the automatic number alone. Reason: once the run was in, the automatic rule had matched 2 of 38 moments, so confirmed coverage could not exceed 10 of 38 (26%) whatever the labels; validity and verdict agreement would describe a tool that is not close to the target, at the cost of most of the judging time. Measurement: 10 labels made, 1 pair same, 1 related, 0 of 8 cross-category candidates same; 7 decided from the two note texts, 3 by watching. Decision: confirmed coverage reported (1 of 38); validity and verdict agreement reported as not judged; recorded as an amendment to P9, P10 and P14, made after the automatic held-out number was printed and before any label was written.

**D30. What the held-out result says.** Measurement: confirmed coverage 1 of 38 (3%, Wilson 0 to 13), the one match a `not_fixed` verdict on a carried note; automatic coverage 2 of 38 in both held-out runs, under an 11% floor; 4 or 5 notes per cut against 7 to 16 strategist notes; the misses are clarity, delivery, continuity and story-logic moments, 30 of 36 of them observable. On the tuning cuts no one-call variant moved the note count (1 to 8 per cut in every arm), windowed passes tripled it without lifting coverage over its floor, and the one clear positive lift (8 points, 3.8 Flash on three tuning cuts) comes from a model that answered 3 of 74 calls. Decision: the write-up reports the gap as measured, with no reframed metric; the next steps are ranked in WRITEUP.md section 4, led by narrower per-question calls aimed at the families the tool misses.
